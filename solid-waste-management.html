Solid Waste Management

We handle municipal solid waste from the doorstep through to processing — primary collection and transportation, source segregation support, micro compost centre and Material Recovery Facility operations, fresh waste processing, and biomining of legacy dumpsites.

01 — Why it matters

Segregation is what makes everything downstream work

A processing facility fed with mixed waste produces very little of value and a great deal of reject. The economics of solid waste management are set at the point of collection, which is why our collection work is built around keeping wet and dry streams genuinely separate rather than simply clearing bins — the outcome SWM Rules 2016 actually requires. On the legacy side, biomining reclaims land a municipality already owns and is otherwise unable to use, and removes a standing environmental liability.

06 — Questions

Solid Waste Management — what we get asked

How is biomining of a legacy dumpsite priced and scheduled?
Pricing is normally per cubic metre or per tonne of legacy waste processed, so the first step is quantifying the dump — area, depth and density, established by survey rather than estimate, because volume assumptions are where these contracts go wrong. Duration depends on volume, screening capacity deployed and monsoon interruption. We quote against a measured volume, not a guessed one.
What fractions are recovered from legacy waste, and where do they go?
Typically soil-like fine fraction, combustible RDF fraction, inert construction material and recoverable recyclables, with proportions varying considerably by dumpsite age and composition. Each fraction needs a confirmed destination before work starts — RDF offtake in particular — and that destination should be written into the contract, not assumed.
Do you supply the vehicles and manpower, or does the ULB provide them?
Either arrangement works and it is set by the tender. We can deploy vehicles, equipment and sanitation manpower as part of scope, or operate assets the ULB already owns. Where we operate ULB vehicles we agree the maintenance and breakdown boundary in writing before mobilisation.
Can you help a town panchayat meet SWM Rules 2016 compliance?
Yes. The practical gaps are usually segregation at source, functioning processing capacity for wet waste, a route for dry recoverables and closure of the legacy dump. We scope work against those gaps and against what a body of that size can realistically sustain in operating cost after the contract ends.
